What the HECVAT Is

The Higher Education Community Vendor Assessment Toolkit (HECVAT) is a standardized questionnaire, maintained by EDUCAUSE and the HEISC Shared Assessments Working Group, that colleges and universities use to evaluate the security and privacy practices of cloud vendors. Completing one lets an institution reuse a single review instead of sending us a bespoke security questionnaire.

We have completed the HECVAT — Lite (version 2.11), the short form intended for lower-risk services and smaller vendors. It is available below as an Excel workbook, and the full set of answers is reproduced on this page so you can read them without downloading anything.

How to Read Our Answers

Runestone Academy is a small, open source, non-profit project with one full-time employee and a community of volunteer contributors. We have been providing free textbooks as a service since 2012. Several HECVAT questions assume a vendor with a dedicated information security office, a formal audit budget, and a multi-person operations team; where that is not true of us we answer No rather than stretching the truth.

From our answer to DOCU-01: “Runestone Academy is a small non-profit open source project. There is exactly one person with access to the very minimal amount of PII we store for a student… As a long time professor I take FERPA and student privacy very seriously, but I have to be pragmatic. If we grow beyond this we will of course adopt the appropriate protocols and standards.”

It also helps to know what data is actually at stake. We store a username, email address, and first and last name; the email address is used only for password resets and the name is used only by the instructor of a course. We also store the answers students submit and the code they write. We store no financial information and no confidential grade information beyond course work submitted in the platform.

Where we are strong

  • Data is encrypted in transit and at rest, including at the database layer.
  • Institution data stays in the USA data zone; backups do not leave it.
  • Role-based access control for both end users and administrators, plus audit logging of logins, logouts, actions, and source IP addresses.
  • All servers sit on private IPs inside a virtual private cloud behind multiple firewalls; traffic is accepted only through CloudFlare, which also provides bot and persistent-threat mitigation.
  • Dependencies and container images are scanned continuously with Snyk and GitHub Dependabot, and fixes ship before releases.
  • A documented, followed change management process, including handling of emergency changes.

Where we have gaps

  • No SSAE 18, CSA STAR, CAIQ, or FISMA attestation, and no formal conformance claim against NIST CSF or ISO 27001.
  • No documented Business Continuity Plan or Disaster Recovery Plan.
  • No formal, written incident response plan.
  • No dedicated information security staff or office — and no third-party security assessment within the last year.
  • Multi-tenant environment: institution data is logically scoped by course, not physically separated per customer.

Our Data Security & Privacy Plan describes the safeguards we do have in place, and our Accessibility & VPAT page covers accessibility conformance.
